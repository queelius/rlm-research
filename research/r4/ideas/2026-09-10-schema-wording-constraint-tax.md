## What the papers actually establish

**Schema Key Wording.** Seven models span Qwen2.5-3B/7B/Coder-14B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, and Llama 1B/3B/8B. GSM8K/Math500 accuracy is compared under four instruction placements: neither, key, prompt, both; XGrammar and field count/order remain fixed. Reasoning-field names acquire instruction-like wording. Qwen2.5-7B GSM8K rises 79.61→86.50 under key-only, but the distilled model falls 61.18→49.89: the family headline is not universal. The reported intervention is not record-specific reference substitution. Limitations include mathematical tasks and no internal causal explanation. The inspected text does not supply literal complete key/prompt variants, exact sampling/caps, run-level uncertainty or a realized token/GPU ledger; attribution and reproducibility therefore remain bounded. No equal-token control is established by equal field structure. [Primary paper, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14862v1).

**Constraint Tax.** Main coverage is Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B and SmolLM2-1.7B, five synthetic families, 1,000 instances/model/mode and 15,000 generations; Qwen2.5-3B is a boundary check. It separates normalized-answer, executable, validity and wrong-valid metrics, using paired-instance bootstrap intervals where available. Its tax clips gains to zero. The calendar Qwen1.5B comparison has 200 instances/mode: both fully valid, executable accuracy 91.5% prompt-JSON versus 48.0% hard schema. Delayed calendar packaging deterministically reserializes the first-stage objects; it is not independent sampled improvement. Calendar latency is 1.63s versus 1.69s, not a matched total-token proof. The smaller MLX study and vLLM/SGLang comparisons have distinct scope/backend caveats; neither synthetic tasks nor the 3B boundary establish broad agent transfer or a scaling law. A full attempted-request/GPU ledger and equal-compute delayed-generation control are not supplied by these tables. [Primary paper, §§3–9 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26128v1).

## Our permissible claim and controls

Do **not** claim first discovery that schema language influences semantics, grammar can damage correctness, format and content need separate scores, or solving before packaging helps. Those are prior directions, also reflected in the earlier DCCD/Where-vs-What note. Neither paper establishes equivalence between unrelated aliases and matching IDs, nor the causal origin of our model's behavior.

The narrower candidate is **record-specific reference interference**: with unchanged field names and exact tag grammar, does a requested tag naming another visible record alter the displayed record's semantic classification more than an unrelated alias? This is a controlled behavioral question, not an internal binding-mechanism identification or a priority claim against all literature. Our requested tags change both prompt content and forced emitted values; the present experiment does not isolate those channels.

Our current controls preserve complete MNLI texts, fixed label-blind selection, 16 context clusters, paired seeds, the complete 48-row contract and 768 planned labels/arm. Native NULL bounds remain separate from known malformed zero scores and shape-conditional diagnostics. Only full-contract-valid shifted positions with unequal displayed/named gold enter the named-record diagnostic. This guards denominators; it does not make exposed task design confirmatory.

Alien strings are length-matched, unique and collision-checked in the named inventory. They control additional forced tag-token burden better than a label-only baseline; they are not semantically neutral filler by proof. Repeated constant tags differ in repetition as well as correspondence. Equal token counts do not equal lexical familiarity, entropy, attention or computation. Alien-minus-matching must remain signed with uncertainty, not declared equivalence from nonsignificance. No paper or leaf-only result establishes improvements in optional child acquisition, faithful root reduction or larger-model/native-RLM task success.
