# Are row anchors keys, or just counting aids?

The 16-context field-order result replicated the selective effect directionally: +9.77 percentage
points on the aligned-controlled interaction, positive in 14/16 contexts. A separate 16-context
positional-anchor study produced a much larger late-position interaction, +657/1,536 or +42.77
points, positive in every context. Its erratum matters: each late cell contains 512 labels (16 calls
times 32 positions), not 384; all recorded effects already used the correct denominator.

The smallest clean next test that also prepares a composition interface is a 16-context, 192-call
factorial. Cross wrong, unrelated-alien, and aligned visible references with four output packages:
labels only; ordinary sequential rows 0–47; a fixed permutation of numeric keys 0–47 attached to
records; and fixed opaque per-record keys. Every keyed output echoes its input key before the label
and remains in displayed record order. Sequential rows permit ordinary counting. Permuted numbers
preserve unique correspondence while breaking ordinal alignment. Opaque keys remove numeric order.

This is not another shifted-ID grid. The completed shift controls asked for another visible public
record ID and showed that the model often followed the record named by that ID. Here, neither stable
key can name a public record: permuted numbers are arbitrary assignments, and opaque keys live in a
distinct `k`+12-hex namespace from the public `m`+12-hex IDs. The mapping is fixed before outcomes
from hashes of the master seed, context manifest, and public record ID. This directly tests whether
the ordered 0–47 effect survives when ordinal counting is unavailable. Batch-prompting literature
and the earlier cards already motivate indexing and permutation controls; there is no novelty claim.

The primary quantity is late-position stable-anchor retention: the average gain of permuted and
opaque keys over labels-only, minus the sequential-row gain, averaged over the three references and
16 context units. This pooled contrast stays descriptive, but cannot promote opaque keys by hiding a
weak opaque arm behind a strong numeric one. Promote opaque keys to a separately designed downstream
join task only if all cells retain at least 15/16 contexts, opaque alone gains at least 25 points over
labels-only, trails sequential rows by no more than 10 points, is positive in at least 12/16 contexts,
and has no material contract or availability disadvantage. Report the identical gate separately for
permuted numeric keys. Favor a counting explanation only if sequential rows beat each stable condition by at
least 15 points and stable keys average no more than a 10-point gain. Anything between is mixed and
does not yet justify a new root domain.

Contexts are the clustered paired units. The prompt/schema packages differ, and “new” means only
inventory-excluded—not pretraining-unseen. One seed per context limits sampling claims. An opaque-key
pass would license a later composition design using those keys as joins; it would not itself prove a
binding or attention mechanism. Opaque and numeric strings may tokenize differently, so report
condition-level token usage and key-copy validity; this is an encoding-package comparison, not a
tokenization-isolated causal effect. Structured decoding forces the key-bearing grammar and can
supply key-token continuations, so exact key validity is only a contract diagnostic—not evidence of
freely learned key retrieval or copying.
