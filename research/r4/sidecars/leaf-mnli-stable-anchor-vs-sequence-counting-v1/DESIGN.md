# Stable anchor versus sequence counting: frozen design

This prospective leaf-only study asks whether the replicated late-position benefit of ordered row
numbers survives when every record still has a unique visible correspondence key but the key no
longer equals sequence position. It is an encoding-package comparison, not proof of a binding,
attention, copying, or counting mechanism.

The fixed 192 calls pair 16 new contexts across three reference conditions (wrong, alien, aligned)
and four grammars (labels only, sequential numeric, permuted numeric, opaque). Output order always
equals displayed record order. Numeric permutation and opaque assignment are fixed from master seed
998317001 before outcomes. Opaque keys use `k` plus 12 hex characters and cannot equal any public,
shifted, or alien `m` plus 12-hex record ID. Eligibility uses gold labels only to construct complete,
conflict-free three-label premise groups; selection rank does not use labels, gold, length, or
outcomes. Inventory exclusions are frozen before selection. If any genre has fewer than 64 eligible
groups, preparation stops and preserves the insufficiency receipt.

The primary descriptive quantity is the late-position pooled stable-key gain over labels-only minus
the sequential gain. Downstream opaque-key promotion is deliberately separate: opaque alone must
gain at least 25 percentage points over labels-only, be within 10 points of sequential, be positive
in at least 12/16 context units, retain at least 15/16 available calls in every cell, and have no
contract/availability disadvantage. Report the same gate independently for permuted numeric keys.
No outcome may revise these gates.

Structured decoding forces key-bearing output grammar and may supply key-token continuations. Key
fidelity is therefore only a contract diagnostic. It cannot establish freely learned retrieval or
copying. Token lengths and usage are reported because the grammars are not tokenization-matched.

Use released base Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 revision
`cdbee75f17c01a7cc42f958dc650907174af0554`, one A100, four workers, 90 seconds/request, and an
1,800-second outer cap. Retain all endpoints, including NULL and invalid outcomes. No retries,
repairs, rerolls, tools, adapters, training, or downstream root task are allowed.
