# Stable keys recover late-batch accuracy without encoding sequence position

The exact owner terminal and parent EXIT authenticate a complete, released run with exit 0, no
timeout, and an empty GPU inventory. The released-base Qwen3-4B service used no adapter or prefix
cache. Independent replay accounted for 192/192 physical requests, returned responses, unique
provider request IDs, and native-authenticated completions. Every whole-output contract was valid;
there were no invalid observed answers, NULLs, unexpected call directories, unknown usage cells, or
disagreements with producer scores.

Late-position accuracy pooled across wrong, alien, and aligned reference conditions was 525/1,536
(34.18%) for labels only, 1,312/1,536 (85.42%) for sequential numeric keys, 1,323/1,536 (86.13%)
for permuted numeric keys, and 1,304/1,536 (84.90%) for opaque keys. Thus the pooled stable-key
conditions gain 51.33 percentage points over labels only and differ from sequential numbering by
only +0.10 point. This answers the prospective question: the large late-position benefit survives
when unique keys do not encode display sequence.

Both independently frozen promotion gates pass. Opaque keys gain 779/1,536 late labels, or +50.72
points over labels only, and are only 0.52 point below sequential numbering. Permuted numeric keys
gain 798/1,536, or +51.95 points, and are 0.72 point above sequential. Each effect is positive in
16/16 paired contexts; every relation-by-anchor cell has 16/16 available and contract-valid calls.
With no missing outcomes, paired-known estimates and planned-denominator bounds coincide.

The scientific conclusion is narrower than a mechanism claim. Stable per-record anchors are a
reusable alternative to ordinal row numbers under this forced structured-output contract; simple
sequence counting is not necessary for the observed repair. Structured decoding may supply key
continuations, and output order remains fixed to display order, so the result does not prove freely
learned binding, copying, attention, or counting. Opaque keys are also much longer in tokens than
numeric keys, so the study is not tokenization-matched.

The 16 newly selected MNLI contexts exclude named prior research inventories, but the task/model
are familiar and the contexts are not guaranteed pretraining-unseen. Context—not any of the 4,608
late labels—is the paired unit; there is one stochastic seed per context and no repeated-sampling
estimate. Results pool three reference conditions and apply to one released 4B model.

Known local usage was 787,869 input, 100,581 output, and zero cached tokens across 192 new calls.
There were no shared or reused calls. Provider billing remains unknown. The auditor authored the
producer's two prelaunch scoring amendments, but not the main producer; the outcome reader
independently reimplemented contract parsing, native authentication, and clustered aggregation.
